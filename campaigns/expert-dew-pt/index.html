Common Problems

Moisture Damages Your Compressed Air System

High moisture content leads to corrosion, equipment failures, and production quality issues. Here's what happens when dew point isn't properly monitored.

Corrosion & Rust Formation

Steel compressed air lines corrode rapidly above 50% relative humidity. Rust particles break off, travel through the system, and block nozzles and control elements, causing expensive repairs and downtime.

Equipment Damage

Moisture causes pneumatic tools and machinery to malfunction. Water droplets contaminate lubricants, damage seals, and lead to premature component failure and costly maintenance.

Production Quality Issues

In painting and coating applications, moisture creates bubbles and surface defects. Hygroscopic products like spices and sugar clump together during pneumatic transport, affecting product quality.

Energy Waste

A 30 kW compressor can release 0.7 CF of water into the compressed air line in just 8 hours at 60% humidity and 68°F. This represents significant energy waste and system inefficiency.
